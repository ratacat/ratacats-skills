# Lens Pack: Inquiry

Mode: falsify. Targets: claims, arguments, plans, specs, essays, decisions, research questions, models of how something works — non-code targets where the ask is "is this true / sound / complete".

Probe grammar: conjectures — specific, testable claims about where the target is wrong, weak, or incomplete ("the plan's timeline assumes the vendor API exists; it doesn't"). Evidence comes from sources, documents, data, calculation, or tight reasoning chains; name the evidence type on every closure. In this pack, `confirmed` means a real weakness or falsehood; `intentional` means a deliberate, acknowledged tradeoff.

## Lenses

Generate 1-5 conjectures per applicable lens unless the user requests a different distribution.

1. Load-bearing claims — which statements, if false, collapse the whole; test those first
2. Definition drift — key terms that shift meaning between sections or speakers
3. Causal leaps — correlation read as cause; mechanism asserted but never shown
4. Base rates — claimed effect sizes or odds versus the reference class
5. Counterexample hunt — one concrete case that breaks the general claim
6. Source quality — who says so, what they'd gain, what they could actually observe
7. Missing stakeholder — whose costs or knowledge the argument never prices in
8. Unstated assumptions — premises the conclusion needs but the text never defends
9. Internal consistency — sections, numbers, or commitments that contradict each other
10. Scope creep / scope shrink — conclusions broader or narrower than the evidence
11. Alternative explanation — a rival hypothesis the evidence fits equally well
12. Failure path — the concrete sequence by which the plan or claim fails in practice
13. Falsifiability — what observation would change the author's mind; does one exist
14. Staleness — facts, prices, laws, or APIs that have changed since the target was written

## Operator Hints

Operators that pay off most on claims and plans:
- **Inversion** — steelman the opposite conclusion from the same evidence.
- **Extremization** — apply the claim at its boundary: smallest case, largest case, adversarial actor.
- **Time shift** — did this hold five years ago; what breaks it five years out.
- **Recombination** — combine two confirmed weaknesses; check whether they compound.
